\section*{Impact Statement (40 words max)}

% 36 words (scripts/count_words.py)
Photogrammetric meshes of brain dissections are attractive references for
diffusion MRI, but they are rarely closed volumes. We show what automatic
closing achieves, where it fails, and why input mesh quality, not closing,
limits quantitative use.

% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
\section*{Synopsis (100 words max)}

% Motivation / Goals / Approach / Results
Motivation: photogrammetric meshes of fibre dissections could serve as
quantitative anatomical references for diffusion MRI, but they are open and
defective. Goals: close them automatically into watertight volumes
and measure how far these volumes can be trusted. Approach: a repair and
capping pipeline (planar caps, curved caps for folded openings) applied to
all 130 epochs of the 12 public BraDiPho specimens, checked numerically and
visually. Results: all epochs became watertight, and the capping choice
changed volumes by 0.45\% on average. However, volume increased in 15 of
104 dissection steps, by up to 15\%, mostly because the scanned boundary
moved.

% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
\section*{Introduction}

Fibre dissection combined with photogrammetry produces textured 3D meshes of
the brain at successive dissection steps (``epochs''). The BraDiPho archive$^{1}$ makes them public for comparison with diffusion MRI
tractography. Quantitative use (volumes, volume removed per step,
registration to MRI) requires watertight surfaces, but the meshes are open on
the face that was not photographed and contain scanning defects (duplicated
and inverted faces, flaps, non-manifold edges, debris). We closed the entire
archive automatically and asked: are the volumes good enough to measure what
the dissection removed? We report what works, what does not, and what we wish
we had known at the start.

% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
\section*{Methods}

\textbf{Data.} All 130 epochs of the 12 BraDiPho specimens; ``b'' variants,
whose meaning is undocumented, were excluded from comparisons between epochs.

\textbf{Pipeline} (Python, VTK, Triangle). (1) Repair: duplicated faces,
small flaps and open fragments removed; non-manifold edges split; orientation
made consistent and outward. (2) Boundary loops extracted. (3) For each loop,
a plane is fitted by SVD. If the projected loop is a simple polygon (after
straightening micro-folds of up to 20 vertices), a planar cap is triangulated
(constrained Delaunay) on the outer side of the opening and joined to the
loop by a side band; otherwise, a curved cap is built by harmonic
interpolation. (4) Final repairs, removal of debris inside or far from the
brain, outward orientation of each part. (5) Volume is computed only if no
boundary or non-manifold edge remains.

\textbf{Evaluation.} (a) Watertightness. (b) Sensitivity of volume to
capping: comparison with a flat-fan closing, and two fold-straightening
variants (A: always planar; B: planar only if the added wedge is
$\leq$5\%). (c) Visual inspection of every epoch. (d) Plausibility: dissection
only removes tissue, so volume should decrease. Each increase was compared
with control pairs for scale (ICP with scaling), position of the scanned
boundary, and cap wedge.

% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
\section*{Results}

\textbf{What works.} All 130 epochs became watertight (Fig.~1).
Straightening micro-folds reduced epochs needing a curved cap from 38 to 12.
Volume differed from the flat-fan closing by 0.45\% on average (maximum
3.4\%). Visual defects dropped from 29 to 12 epochs.

\textbf{What works poorly.} The 12 remaining defective epochs (7 from one
specimen) have truly folded openings: the curved cap folds or spans beyond
the edge (Fig.~3a). Small holes just under a planar cap leave inverted
patches (Fig.~3b). On four hollowed openings, variants A and B disagreed by
5.7--11\%: volume there depends on an arbitrary choice.

\textbf{What does not work.} Over 104 consecutive-epoch pairs, the median
volume removed was 8.7\% (0.5--37.5\%), but volume \emph{increased} in 15
pairs, by up to 15.3\%, and 21 changes were within capping uncertainty
(Fig.~2). Epochs share one frame (median distance 0.4--0.7~mm); scale
differences ($\leq$0.8\%) did not distinguish increases from controls. In 9
of 15 increases, including the three largest, the scanned boundary lay
2.2--5.1~mm further out, adding 19--57~cm$^3$ (Fig.~3c). Two followed poorly
closed epochs; four were within uncertainty.

% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
\section*{Discussion}

``Watertight'' does not mean ``correct'': a three-vertex micro-fold suffices
to replace a planar cut by a warped membrane, and inverted debris silently
subtracts volume. Only visual inspection revealed these defects. Capping
uncertainty (about 3\%, up to 11\% on hollowed openings) cannot explain
increases of 13--15\%: the scanned boundary itself moves, so any closing
would inherit the shift. Both faces are dissected, so an inward shift can be
real, but an outward shift of 2--5~mm cannot: it reflects tissue settling,
slight specimen motion and independent captures. Even between control epochs
the cap moved by about $\pm$2~mm, and 1~mm costs 2--4\% of volume, comparable
to a typical dissection step, while tissue removed on the unphotographed face
is not measured at all. The limit is the photogrammetric input, not closing.

\textbf{Recommendations.} Photograph both faces at every epoch; fix the
specimen on a rigid support with fiducial markers and a scale reference;
reprocess the original photographs consistently; consider LiDAR or structured
light for future acquisitions; report capping uncertainty and visual checks
with every volume; document the ``b'' epochs.

\textbf{Limitations.} No ground-truth volumes were available, and real
tissue removal on the unphotographed face cannot be separated from settling
or motion with the current data.

% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
\section*{Conclusion}

Photogrammetric dissection meshes can be closed automatically, but their
volumes are trustworthy only to within a few percent, and many dissection
steps are not measurable. Quantitative use requires better control upstream,
at acquisition and reconstruction.
